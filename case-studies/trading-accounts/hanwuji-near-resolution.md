## Strategy economics

`Illustrative only.` Not an @hanwuji fill.

```text
Entry       0.98
Settlement  1.00
----------------
Gross upside 0.02/share
```

Asymmetric downside if the near-certain-looking side is wrong:

```text
Entry       0.98
Wrong resolution
Settlement  0.00
----------------
Loss        0.98/share
```

A pattern that repeatedly collects 1–5¢ can still take a large loss when a near-certain-looking outcome reverses. Success is not implied by a high historical win rate, and this page does not compute one.

At the time reviewed, the profile displayed positive Past Day P/L. Several reviewed resolved positions show small gains from high-price entries that settled at $1. That is not a statement that the account or “the bot” is profitable.

## Risk / reward structure

The observable pattern suggests an exchange of **high probability / small payoff** for **low probability / potentially large loss**:

```text
Many small wins
        vs.
Rare but large failure
```

That makes risk management central. Relevant failure modes in this family include:

- False certainty near resolution
- Final-second reversal
- Oracle or reference-price uncertainty
- Spread, liquidity, and late fills
- Stale market data
- Resolution-rule differences
- Market suspension
- Fees
- Execution latency

This page does not claim which controls @hanwuji uses.

## What PolyTutor learns from this pattern

Public-account analysis raises engineering questions. It does not answer them with proprietary parameters.

- When is a 98¢ outcome actually attractive after fees and flip risk?
- How should probability confidence be estimated?
- How much time must remain?
- When does expected value disappear after spread and fees?
- How does book depth change the average entry?
- How quickly can a near-certain market reverse?
- What maximum exposure is appropriate?
- How should correlated BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP positions be handled?
- What happens if the executable price moves from 96¢ to 99¢ before the order fills?

> **Direction alone is not an edge. Execution is part of the strategy.** A correct late-side call at a worse fill is a different trade.
